# Strategy to Mine Listening Resources

Listening is the main work. Speaking grows out of it: you can only say what you've heard many times first.

## Where to find listening material

Start where you understand **about 70–80%** without pausing.

| Level | Source | Why |
|---|---|---|
| **Beginner (A1–A2)** | **Nicos Weg** (Deutsche Welle, free) | A story-based course with videos, subtitles and exercises. Works as a course *and* as listening practice |
| | **Easy German** (YouTube) | Street interviews in Berlin and other cities, with German + English subtitles. Real people, real speed |
| | **Die Sendung mit der Maus**, **Peppa Wutz** | Children's shows. Simple, clear speech, lots of everyday words |
| | **Slow German**, **Coffee Break German** (podcasts) | Slower speech, made for learners |
| **Intermediate (A2–B1)** | **Langsam gesprochene Nachrichten** (DW) | Daily news read slowly, with a transcript |
| | **nachrichtenleicht.de** (Deutschlandfunk) | News in simple German, with audio |
| | **logo!** (ZDF) | Children's news. Short and clear |
| | **ARD / ZDF Mediathek** | Free German TV online, often with German subtitles |
| **B1 and up** | **Netflix German originals** | Dark, Kleo, How to Sell Drugs Online (Fast), Babylon Berlin |
| | **Twitch / YouTube in German** | Gaming streams (MLBB, Dota) and tech channels. Hobby content keeps you watching |
| | **Berlin – Tag & Nacht** | A trashy reality-style soap with very colloquial Berlin German |

**Tool:** **Language Reactor** is a browser extension for Netflix and YouTube. It shows German and English subtitles together, lets you pause after each line, and saves words.

## How to mine it: two modes

### Mode 1: lots of listening (most of your time)

- Listen a lot and **don't stop** to look things up.
- The goal is volume. Your ear gets used to the speed and sound of German.
- Use commutes, cooking, the gym.
- If you understand less than half, switch to something easier.

### Mode 2: intensive mining (10–20 min a day, 1 short clip)

1. **Listen blind:** play a 1–3 minute clip once, with no subtitles. Note roughly what you understood.
2. **Listen with German subtitles:** pause on each line you missed. Is it a word you don't know, or one you know but didn't recognise when spoken? The second kind is more common, and more useful to spot.
3. **Mine it:** pick **3–5 useful chunks**, whole phrases rather than single words, that you'd hear or say in your life. Add them to the matching context folder:
   - something someone said → `phrase_to_hear.csv`, with `who`
   - something you'd want to say → `phrase_to_say.csv`, with `who`
   - a new word → `vocab_*.csv`, with `similarity`
4. **Shadow it:** replay the lines you mined and repeat them out loud, copying the rhythm.
5. **Listen blind again:** you'll catch much more the second time.
6. **Review later:** the mined phrases now go into your normal review in `strategy-to-retrieve.md` (1 day, 3 days, 1 week).

## Choosing what to mine

- **Pick phrases you'll actually meet.** Easy German interviews at a Späti beat a news story about politics.
- **Pick phrases, not single words.** "Hast du Bock mitzukommen?" is worth more than "Bock".
- **Pick things you've heard more than once.** If a phrase keeps coming up, it's important.
- **Skip the rest.** You don't need every unknown word. More listening will fill the gaps.

## A simple weekly rhythm

| Day | Lots of listening | Intensive mining |
|---|---|---|
| Mon–Fri | 30–60 min (podcast, Easy German) | 1 clip, 3–5 chunks |
| Weekend | One episode of a series with German subtitles | Review the week's mined chunks |

## Check your progress

Every 4–6 weeks, watch the same Easy German street interview you watched at the start. If you understand more each time, the plan is working.
