# Subordinate Clauses

## What is this about?

So far, your sentences have been simple: one subject, one verb, one idea.

- Ich lerne Deutsch.
- Ich wohne in Berlin.

Now you'll learn to **connect ideas** with words like **because**, **that**, **if**, **whether**:

- Ich lerne Deutsch, **weil** ich in Berlin **wohne**. (I learn German because I live in Berlin.)

## Main clause vs subordinate clause

- **Main clause** (Hauptsatz): can stand alone. "Ich lerne Deutsch."
- **Subordinate clause** (Nebensatz): can't stand alone, it depends on the main clause. "...weil ich in Berlin wohne."

"Because I live in Berlin." on its own feels incomplete, right? That's a subordinate clause.

## The big rule: verb goes to the END

Here's the surprise. In a subordinate clause, the conjugated verb **jumps to the very end**:

- Main clause: Ich **wohne** in Berlin. (verb in seat 2)
- Subordinate: ..., weil ich in Berlin **wohne**. (verb at the end)

Compare to English: "because I **live** in Berlin" — English doesn't move anything. German does.

## Why it matters

This is the step from "beginner sentences" to "real sentences". Without it, you can only say short, choppy things. With it, you can explain reasons, give opinions, and talk about conditions.

- Ich glaube, **dass** das richtig ist. (I think that's right.)
- **Wenn** ich Zeit habe, komme ich. (If I have time, I'll come.)
- Ich weiß nicht, **ob** er kommt. (I don't know if he's coming.)
