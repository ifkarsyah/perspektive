# Two-Way Prepositions

## What is this about?

So far you have:
- prepositions that **always** take accusative (für, ohne...)
- prepositions that **always** take dative (mit, zu, bei...)

Now a third group that can take **either one**. They're called **two-way prepositions** (Wechselpräpositionen).

**an, auf, hinter, in, neben, über, unter, vor, zwischen**

## How do you choose?

It depends on whether something is **moving to a place** or **staying in a place**:

| Question                     | Situation          | Case            |
| ---------------------------- | ------------------ | --------------- |
| **wohin?** (where to?)       | movement, change of place | **accusative** |
| **wo?** (where?)             | location, no change | **dative**     |

## Compare with English

English sometimes shows this difference with two words:

- I go **into** the room. (movement) → Ich gehe **in das** Zimmer. (accusative)
- I am **in** the room. (location) → Ich bin **in dem** Zimmer. (dative)

"into" vs "in", "onto" vs "on". German uses the same preposition but changes the case instead.

## Picture it

Imagine a ball and a box:

- 🏀 ➡️ 📦 The ball **flies into** the box → **wohin?** → accusative: **in den** Karton
- 🏀📦 The ball **is in** the box → **wo?** → dative: **in dem** (im) Karton

## Why it matters

These prepositions are everywhere when you talk about places: putting things somewhere, saying where things are, giving directions, describing your apartment.
