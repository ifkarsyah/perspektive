# Perfekt (Spoken Past Tense)

## What is this about?

How to talk about the **past**: what you did yesterday, last weekend, on vacation.

## Compare with English

English has several past tenses:

- I **ate** pizza. (simple past)
- I **have eaten** pizza. (present perfect)

German also has two, but they're used differently:

- **Perfekt**: Ich **habe** Pizza **gegessen**. → used when **speaking**
- **Präteritum**: Ich **aß** Pizza. → used mostly in **writing** (books, news)

The good news: **in everyday conversation, Germans use the Perfekt for almost everything.** So this one tense covers both "I ate" and "I have eaten".

| English                       | German (Perfekt)               |
| ----------------------------- | ------------------------------ |
| I ate pizza.                  | Ich habe Pizza gegessen.       |
| I have eaten pizza.           | Ich habe Pizza gegessen.       |
| I went home.                  | Ich bin nach Hause gegangen.   |
| Did you sleep well?           | Hast du gut geschlafen?        |

## How it's built: two parts

The Perfekt looks like the English "have eaten" — a helper verb + a past form:

> Ich **habe** Pizza **gegessen**.
> I **have** pizza **eaten**.

1. **Helper verb**: **haben** or **sein**, conjugated, in seat 2.
2. **Past participle** (Partizip II): like "eaten", "gone", "played". Goes to the **end**.

This is the same "sandwich" you know from [modal verbs](../a1/52-infinitive-at-the-end.md):

```
Ich [habe] ...gestern Pizza... [gegessen].
```

## One exception to know now

For **sein**, **haben**, and the **modal verbs**, Germans usually use the Präteritum even when speaking, because it's shorter:

| Instead of...              | People say...       | English       |
| -------------------------- | ------------------- | ------------- |
| Ich bin gewesen.           | Ich **war**.        | I was.        |
| Ich habe gehabt.           | Ich **hatte**.      | I had.        |
| Ich habe gemusst.          | Ich **musste**.     | I had to.     |
| Ich habe gekonnt.          | Ich **konnte**.     | I could.      |
| Ich habe gewollt.          | Ich **wollte**.     | I wanted.     |

- Ich **war** gestern im Kino. (I was at the cinema yesterday.)
- Ich **hatte** keine Zeit. (I had no time.)

## Why it matters

Without the past tense, you can only talk about now. With the Perfekt you can tell stories, talk about your weekend, and answer "What did you do?" — one of the most common questions in any conversation.
