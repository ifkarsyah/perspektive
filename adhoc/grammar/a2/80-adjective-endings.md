# Adjective Endings

## What is this about?

When an adjective (big, red, new...) comes **before a noun**, it needs an ending in German.

- Der Hund ist **groß**. (The dog is big. — adjective after the noun → no ending)
- der **große** Hund (the big dog — adjective before the noun → ending!)
- ein **großer** Hund (a big dog — different ending!)

## The idea in one sentence

If the article already shows the gender and case, the adjective just gets **-e** or **-en**. If not, the adjective has to show it itself.

## Compare with English

English adjectives **never** change:

- the big dog, a big dog, the big dogs, with the big dog — always "big".

German adjectives change depending on:
1. the **gender** of the noun (der/die/das)
2. the **case** (nominative, accusative, dative)
3. **which article** comes before (der, ein, or nothing)

That's a lot of combinations, which is why this topic has a reputation for being hard.

## Let's be honest

This is one of the **hardest** parts of German grammar. Even advanced learners make mistakes here, and it usually takes a long time before it becomes automatic.

So the goal at A2 is **not** perfection. The goal is:

1. Understand the **logic** behind the endings.
2. Know the **most common** combinations.
3. Memorize some **fixed phrases** (Guten Morgen! Schönes Wochenende!).

If you get it wrong when speaking, people will still understand you perfectly.

## The key idea: someone has to show the gender

Here's the secret that makes it all make sense:

> **The article OR the adjective must show the gender/case. If the article already shows it, the adjective takes a lazy ending (-e or -en). If the article doesn't show it, the adjective has to do the work.**

Look:

- **der** Hund → "der" clearly says masculine. Adjective is lazy: der groß**e** Hund.
- **ein** Hund → "ein" could be masculine or neuter. Adjective must show it: ein groß**er** Hund (the -er from **der**).
- Hund (no article) → nothing shows it. Adjective must do it all: groß**er** Hund.

Once you understand this, the tables make sense.

## Situation 1: After der / die / das (the)

### The logic

der, die, das, den, dem already show everything clearly. So the adjective is **lazy** and only uses two endings: **-e** or **-en**.

### The table

| Case | masc.               | fem.                | neut.               | plural                |
| ---- | ------------------- | ------------------- | ------------------- | --------------------- |
| Nom. | der groß**e** Hund  | die groß**e** Katze | das groß**e** Haus  | die groß**en** Hunde  |
| Akk. | den groß**en** Hund | die groß**e** Katze | das groß**e** Haus  | die groß**en** Hunde  |
| Dat. | dem groß**en** Hund | der groß**en** Katze | dem groß**en** Haus | den groß**en** Hunden |

### The shortcut

Only **5 spots** get **-e**. **Everything else** gets **-en**.

The 5 -e spots:
- all three **nominative singular** (der große, die große, das große)
- **accusative feminine and neuter** (die große, das große)

In other words: **-e** where the article is **der, die, or das** (and it's not plural or dative). Everywhere else, **-en**.

Draw it as a shape:

```
        masc.  fem.  neut.  plural
Nom.     -e    -e     -e     -en
Akk.     -en   -e     -e     -en
Dat.     -en   -en    -en    -en
```

The -e spots make an "L" shape (or a little staircase). Everything else is -en.

### Examples

- **Der neue** Kollege ist nett. (The new colleague is nice.)
- Ich kaufe **die rote** Tasche. (I'm buying the red bag.)
- Ich wohne in **dem kleinen** Haus. (I live in the small house.)
- Ich kenne **die neuen** Nachbarn. (I know the new neighbors.)

## Situation 2: After ein / eine (a), also kein and mein

### The logic

ein is the problem. Look at it:

- **ein** Hund (masculine, nominative)
- **ein** Haus (neuter, nominative or accusative)

"ein" looks the same for masculine and neuter! It doesn't show the gender. So in those spots, **the adjective has to show it** by borrowing the ending from der/das:

- ein groß**er** Hund ← -er from **der**
- ein groß**es** Haus ← -es from **das**

Everywhere else, ein already shows enough (einen, einem, einer, eine), so the adjective is lazy again (-e or -en).

### The table

| Case | masc.                  | fem.                  | neut.                 | plural (keine/meine)    |
| ---- | ---------------------- | --------------------- | --------------------- | ----------------------- |
| Nom. | ein groß**er** Hund    | eine groß**e** Katze  | ein groß**es** Haus   | keine groß**en** Hunde  |
| Akk. | einen groß**en** Hund  | eine groß**e** Katze  | ein groß**es** Haus   | keine groß**en** Hunde  |
| Dat. | einem groß**en** Hund  | einer groß**en** Katze | einem groß**en** Haus | keinen groß**en** Hunden |

### The shortcut

It's the **same as the der table**, except in the 3 spots where "ein" has no ending:

- ein groß**er** Hund (masc. Nom.) — shows **der**
- ein groß**es** Haus (neut. Nom.) — shows **das**
- ein groß**es** Haus (neut. Akk.) — shows **das**

### Examples

- Das ist **ein guter** Film. (That's a good film.)
- Ich habe **ein neues** Handy. (I have a new phone.)
- Ich suche **eine kleine** Wohnung. (I'm looking for a small apartment.)
- Er hat **einen neuen** Job. (He has a new job.)
- Ich wohne mit **meinem besten** Freund. (I live with my best friend.)

## Situation 3: No article

### The logic

No article at all = nobody shows the gender. So the adjective takes **the ending of the article itself** (der → -er, die → -e, das → -es, dem → -em...).

### The table

| Case | masc.             | fem.             | neut.            | plural             |
| ---- | ----------------- | ---------------- | ---------------- | ------------------ |
| Nom. | gut**er** Wein    | gut**e** Milch   | gut**es** Brot   | gut**e** Freunde   |
| Akk. | gut**en** Wein    | gut**e** Milch   | gut**es** Brot   | gut**e** Freunde   |
| Dat. | gut**em** Wein    | gut**er** Milch  | gut**em** Brot   | gut**en** Freunden |

Compare with the articles: d**er** → gut**er**, d**ie** → gut**e**, d**as** → gut**es**, d**em** → gut**em**. They match!

### When do you use it?

- Uncountable things: frisches Brot, kalte Milch, heißer Kaffee
- Plurals without an article: neue Schuhe, alte Freunde
- Greetings and wishes (see below)

## Fixed phrases (just memorize these)

These are all in the **accusative** (because of an invisible "I wish you..."):

| German                      | English                      |
| --------------------------- | ---------------------------- |
| Gut**en** Morgen!           | Good morning!                |
| Gut**en** Tag!              | Good day! / Hello!           |
| Gut**en** Abend!            | Good evening!                |
| Gut**e** Nacht!             | Good night!                  |
| Schön**es** Wochenende!     | Have a nice weekend!         |
| Gut**e** Reise!             | Have a good trip!            |
| Viel**en** Dank!            | Thanks a lot!                |
| Herzlich**en** Glückwunsch! | Congratulations!             |
| Frohe Weihnachten!          | Merry Christmas!             |
| Mit freundlich**en** Grüßen | Kind regards (letters/emails) |

You've been using adjective endings all along without knowing it!

## A practical strategy for speaking

When you're speaking and don't have time to think:

1. After **der/die/das** → use **-e** for singular nominative, **-en** for everything else. You'll be right most of the time.
2. After **ein** → masculine: **-er**, neuter: **-es**, feminine: **-e**. (Nominative.)
3. If in doubt → say **-en**. It's the most common ending overall and often right.

## Common mistakes

| ❌ Wrong                  | ✅ Right                  | Why                                  |
| ------------------------ | ------------------------ | ------------------------------------ |
| der großer Hund          | der große Hund           | der already shows it → lazy -e       |
| ein große Hund           | ein großer Hund          | ein doesn't show masc. → -er         |
| ein großer Haus          | ein großes Haus          | neuter → -es                         |
| Gute Morgen!             | Guten Morgen!            | accusative masculine → -en           |
| mit dem großer Hund      | mit dem großen Hund      | dative → -en                         |
| Der Hund ist großer.     | Der Hund ist groß.       | after the noun → no ending           |

## Summary

- After **der/die/das**: only **-e** or **-en** (5 spots -e, rest -en).
- After **ein**: same, but masc. Nom. → **-er**, neut. Nom./Akk. → **-es**.
- **No article**: adjective takes the article's ending (-er, -e, -es, -em...).
- Logic: **someone** has to show the gender. If the article doesn't, the adjective does.
- Don't stress. Memorize fixed phrases and improve over time.
