# Dative

## What is this about?

In A1 you learned two cases:

- **Nominative** = who does it (subject)
- **Accusative** = what it's done to (direct object)

Now the third one:

- **Dative** = **who receives it** or **who benefits** (indirect object)

## Compare with English

Look at this sentence:

> I give **the man** **the book**.

There are two "objects":
- **the book** = the thing being given → accusative
- **the man** = the one receiving it → **dative**

English shows this with word order, or with "to": "I give the book **to the man**."

English pronouns even show a hint of it: "I give **him** the book." German is just more consistent and changes the articles too:

> Ich gebe **dem Mann** das Buch.

"der Mann" became "**dem** Mann" because he's the receiver.

## The question word: wem?

To find the dative, ask **"wem?"** (to whom? / for whom?):

- Ich gebe dem Mann das Buch. → **Wem** gebe ich das Buch? → **dem Mann**.

## Why it matters

The dative is everywhere:
- with many common verbs (helfen, danken, gefallen...)
- after very common prepositions (mit, bei, zu, von, aus...)
- in daily phrases (Wie geht es **dir**? Das gefällt **mir**.)

You've actually already used it: "Wie geht es **Ihnen**?" and "zu**m** Arzt" are dative!

## Harder than accusative, but has patterns

In accusative, only masculine changed. In dative, **every gender changes**. But the endings follow a pattern you can learn in one go.
