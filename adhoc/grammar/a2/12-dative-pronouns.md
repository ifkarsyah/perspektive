# Dative Pronouns

## The idea in one sentence

Pronouns also have a dative form: **mir, dir, ihm, ihr, ihm, uns, euch, ihnen, Ihnen** (to me, to you, to him...).

## Compare with English

English has one object form for both accusative and dative:

- He sees **me**. (accusative)
- He gives **me** the book. (dative)

German has two different forms:

- Er sieht **mich**. (accusative)
- Er gibt **mir** das Buch. (dative)

## The table

| Nominative | Accusative | **Dative** | English (dative)    |
| ---------- | ---------- | ---------- | ------------------- |
| ich        | mich       | **mir**    | (to) me             |
| du         | dich       | **dir**    | (to) you            |
| er         | ihn        | **ihm**    | (to) him            |
| sie        | sie        | **ihr**    | (to) her            |
| es         | es         | **ihm**    | (to) it             |
| wir        | uns        | **uns**    | (to) us             |
| ihr        | euch       | **euch**   | (to) you all        |
| sie        | sie        | **ihnen**  | (to) them           |
| Sie        | Sie        | **Ihnen**  | (to) you (formal)   |

## Memory tricks

- **mich / mir**, **dich / dir**: accusative has **-ch**, dative has **-r**. ("ch" for accusative, "r" for receiver.)
- **ihm** ends in **-m**, like **dem**. (der Mann → dem Mann → ihm)
- **ihr** ends in **-r**, like **der** (feminine dative). (die Frau → der Frau → ihr)
- **ihnen** ends in **-n**, like **den** (plural dative).
- **uns** and **euch** are the same in accusative and dative. Free points!

So the pronoun endings match the article endings: dem → ih**m**, der → ih**r**, den → ihne**n**.

## You already know some!

These are everyday phrases that use dative pronouns:

| German                     | English                  | Literally                 |
| -------------------------- | ------------------------ | ------------------------- |
| Wie geht es **dir**?       | How are you?             | How goes it to you?       |
| Wie geht es **Ihnen**?     | How are you? (formal)    | How goes it to you?       |
| Es geht **mir** gut.       | I'm fine.                | It goes well to me.       |
| Das gefällt **mir**.       | I like that.             | That pleases me.          |
| Das schmeckt **mir**.      | That tastes good (to me). | That tastes to me.       |
| Es tut **mir** leid.       | I'm sorry.               | It does me sorrow.        |
| **Mir** ist kalt.          | I'm cold.                | To me is cold.            |
| Das ist **mir** egal.      | I don't care.            | That is to me equal.      |
| Kannst du **mir** helfen?  | Can you help me?         | Can you help to me?       |

Notice "**Mir** ist kalt" (I'm cold): German doesn't say "Ich bin kalt" — that would mean "I'm a cold person"!

## Two objects: which comes first?

When a sentence has both a dative and an accusative object:

### Rule 1: Two nouns → dative first

- Ich gebe **dem Mann** (Dat.) **das Buch** (Akk.).

### Rule 2: Accusative is a pronoun → it comes first

- Ich gebe **es** (Akk.) **dem Mann** (Dat.).
- Ich gebe **es** (Akk.) **ihm** (Dat.).

### Rule 3: Dative pronoun + accusative noun → pronoun first

- Ich gebe **ihm** (Dat.) **das Buch** (Akk.).

Simple version: **pronouns come before nouns**. If both are pronouns, **accusative first**.

## Common mistakes

| ❌ Wrong                    | ✅ Right                  | Why                              |
| -------------------------- | ------------------------ | -------------------------------- |
| Kannst du mich helfen?     | Kannst du mir helfen?    | helfen takes dative              |
| Wie geht es du?            | Wie geht es dir?         | dative pronoun                   |
| Ich bin kalt.              | Mir ist kalt.            | feeling cold = dative            |
| Das gefällt ich.           | Das gefällt mir.         | gefallen takes dative            |
| Ich gebe ihm es.           | Ich gebe es ihm.         | two pronouns: accusative first   |

## Practice

1. Kannst du ___ (me) helfen?
2. Wie geht es ___ (you, formal)?
3. Das Buch gehört ___ (him).
4. Ich gebe ___ (her) die Blumen.
5. Das Essen schmeckt ___ (us).
6. Ich danke ___ (you, du).
7. Replace "dem Mann" with a pronoun: Ich zeige dem Mann den Weg.
8. Replace both: Ich gebe dem Kind das Buch. (→ es + ihm)

<details>
<summary>Answers</summary>

1. mir
2. Ihnen
3. ihm
4. ihr
5. uns
6. dir
7. Ich zeige ihm den Weg.
8. Ich gebe es ihm.

</details>

## Summary

- Dative pronouns: **mir, dir, ihm, ihr, ihm, uns, euch, ihnen, Ihnen**.
- -ch = accusative (mich, dich), -r = dative (mir, dir).
- Lots of daily phrases: Wie geht es dir? Das gefällt mir. Mir ist kalt.
- Order: pronouns before nouns; two pronouns → accusative first.
