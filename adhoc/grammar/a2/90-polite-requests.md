# Polite Requests

## What is this about?

How to ask for things and ask people to do things **politely**: in shops, restaurants, offices, and with strangers.

## Compare with English

English softens requests by using "would" and "could" instead of "will" and "can":

- **Can** you help me? → **Could** you help me? (more polite)
- I **want** a coffee. → I **would like** a coffee. (more polite)

German does exactly the same thing, with special verb forms:

| Direct                       | Polite                             | English                     |
| ---------------------------- | ---------------------------------- | --------------------------- |
| Ich **will** einen Kaffee.   | Ich **möchte** einen Kaffee.       | I want → I'd like a coffee  |
| Ich **habe**...              | Ich **hätte** gern einen Kaffee.   | I'd like to have a coffee   |
| **Kannst** du mir helfen?    | **Könntest** du mir helfen?        | Can you → Could you help me? |
| **Machst** du das Fenster zu? | **Würdest** du das Fenster zumachen? | Will you → Would you close the window? |

These polite forms are called **Konjunktiv II** (subjunctive). You don't need to know the grammar name. Just learn these four words:

- **möchte** (would like)
- **hätte** (would have)
- **könnte** (could)
- **würde** (would)

## Why it matters

Germans can sound quite direct, but in service situations and with strangers, these polite forms are expected. "Ich will ein Bier" in a restaurant sounds demanding. "Ich hätte gern ein Bier" sounds natural and friendly.

Plus, these four words are **the same in every situation**, so a little learning goes a long way.
