# Accusative Prepositions

## The idea in one sentence

After **durch, ohne, gegen, für, um**, the noun is **always accusative**, which means only masculine changes (der → den, ein → einen).

## Prepositions decide the case

Prepositions are small words like **for**, **without**, **through**. In German, a preposition **decides the case** of the noun after it.

In [der and den](30-nominative-and-accusative/31-der-den.md) you learned the case depends on the noun's job (subject or object). Prepositions are a shortcut: **certain prepositions always force the accusative**, no matter what.

## Compare with English

English does this too, with pronouns:

- This is **for me**. (not "for I")
- Without **him**. (not "without he")

After a preposition, English uses the object form (me, him, her). German does the same thing, but also with articles:

- für **den** Mann (for the man) — not "für der Mann"

## Why it matters

"für" and "ohne" especially come up all the time: "Is this for me?", "Coffee without sugar", "for my mother". And the rule is simple: these five **always** take the accusative.

## Step 1: What each one means

| Preposition | Meaning                  | Example                                | English                         |
| ----------- | ------------------------ | -------------------------------------- | ------------------------------- |
| **für**     | for                      | Das Geschenk ist für meinen Vater.     | The gift is for my father.      |
| **ohne**    | without                  | Ich trinke Kaffee ohne Zucker.         | I drink coffee without sugar.   |
| **durch**   | through                  | Wir gehen durch den Park.              | We walk through the park.       |
| **gegen**   | against; around (time)   | Ich bin gegen den Plan. / gegen 8 Uhr  | I'm against the plan. / around 8 |
| **um**      | around (place); at (clock) | Wir sitzen um den Tisch. / um 8 Uhr  | We sit around the table. / at 8 |

Memory trick: **DOGFU** = **D**urch, **O**hne, **G**egen, **F**ür, **U**m.

## Step 2: Only masculine changes

You already know this from [der and den](30-nominative-and-accusative/31-der-den.md). After these prepositions:

| Gender     | Nominative | After für (accusative) |
| ---------- | ---------- | ---------------------- |
| masculine  | der Mann   | für **den** Mann       |
| masculine  | ein Mann   | für **einen** Mann     |
| masculine  | mein Mann  | für **meinen** Mann    |
| feminine   | die Frau   | für die Frau (same)    |
| neuter     | das Kind   | für das Kind (same)    |
| plural     | die Kinder | für die Kinder (same)  |

So in practice: **if the noun is masculine, add -n / -en. Otherwise, nothing changes.**

## Step 3: With pronouns

This is where you'll use them most. After these prepositions, use the accusative pronoun:

| Nominative | After für    | English      |
| ---------- | ------------ | ------------ |
| ich        | für **mich** | for me       |
| du         | für **dich** | for you      |
| er         | für **ihn**  | for him      |
| sie        | für **sie**  | for her      |
| es         | für **es**   | for it       |
| wir        | für **uns**  | for us       |
| ihr        | für **euch** | for you all  |
| sie        | für **sie**  | for them     |
| Sie        | für **Sie**  | for you (formal) |

Compare with English: I → me, he → him. German: ich → **mich**, er → **ihn**. Same idea!

- Ist das **für mich**? (Is that for me?)
- **Ohne dich** gehe ich nicht. (I'm not going without you.)
- Ich habe ein Geschenk **für ihn**. (I have a gift for him.)

## Step 4: Worked example

"I buy a present for my brother." (der Bruder)

1. Preposition: for = **für** → accusative
2. Gender: **der** Bruder → masculine → changes
3. "my" in masculine accusative → **meinen**
4. **Ich kaufe ein Geschenk für meinen Bruder.**

## Two meanings of um

You've seen **um** before in [time expressions](70-time-expressions.md):

- **um** 8 Uhr = **at** 8 o'clock (time)
- **um** den Tisch = **around** the table (place)

Both are correct, just different uses.

## Spoken contractions

In casual speech, people sometimes merge them with das:

- durch das → **durchs**
- für das → **fürs**
- um das → **ums**

You'll hear these, but you don't need to use them.

## Common mistakes

| ❌ Wrong                         | ✅ Right                          | Why                            |
| ------------------------------- | -------------------------------- | ------------------------------ |
| für der Mann                    | für den Mann                     | für + accusative               |
| für mein Vater                  | für meinen Vater                 | masculine → -en                |
| Ist das für ich?                | Ist das für mich?                | accusative pronoun             |
| ohne er                         | ohne ihn                         | er → ihn                       |
| für meinen Mutter               | für meine Mutter                 | feminine doesn't take -en      |

## Summary

- **DOGFU**: durch, ohne, gegen, für, um → always accusative.
- Only masculine changes: den, einen, meinen.
- Pronouns: für mich, für dich, für ihn, für uns...
- um = "at" for clock times and "around" for places.
