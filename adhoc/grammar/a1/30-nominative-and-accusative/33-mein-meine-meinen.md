# mein/meine/meinen

## The idea in one sentence

Words like **mein** (my), **dein** (your), **sein** (his) take **the same endings as ein**, so if you know ein/eine/einen, you already know these.

## Step 1: The possessive words

First, learn which word goes with which person:

| Person          | Possessive | English         | Memory help                    |
| --------------- | ---------- | --------------- | ------------------------------ |
| ich             | **mein**   | my              | sounds like "mine"             |
| du              | **dein**   | your            | d for du                       |
| er              | **sein**   | his             | s... (sein = his, not "to be" here) |
| sie (she)       | **ihr**    | her             | "ihr" sounds like "here"       |
| es              | **sein**   | its             | same as er                     |
| wir             | **unser**  | our             | sounds like "our"              |
| ihr (you all)   | **euer**   | your (all)      |                                |
| sie (they)      | **ihr**    | their           | same as "her"                  |
| Sie (formal)    | **Ihr**    | your (formal)   | capital I, like capital Sie    |

Two traps:
- **sein** also means "to be". Context tells you which.
- **ihr** means "her", "their", AND "you all". Context again.

## Step 2: The ending follows ein

Take the word from step 1, then add the **same ending ein would get**:

| Case        | masc.        | fem.       | neut.      | plural      |
| ----------- | ------------ | ---------- | ---------- | ----------- |
| Nominative  | mein         | mein**e**  | mein       | mein**e**   |
| Accusative  | mein**en**   | mein**e**  | mein       | mein**e**   |

Compare with ein:

| Case        | masc.      | fem.   | neut. |
| ----------- | ---------- | ------ | ----- |
| Nominative  | ein        | eine   | ein   |
| Accusative  | einen      | eine   | ein   |

Exactly the same! The only extra: mein **has a plural** (meine), because "my books" makes sense, but "a books" doesn't.

## Step 3: The ending depends on the thing, not the owner

This is the confusing part for English speakers.

"Anna is looking for her key."

1. Owner is Anna (she) → **ihr**
2. Thing is **der** Schlüssel (key), and it's the object → masculine accusative → **-en**
3. ihr + en = **ihren**
4. **Anna sucht ihren Schlüssel.**

"Peter loves his cat."

1. Owner is Peter (he) → **sein**
2. Thing is **die** Katze, object → feminine → **-e**
3. sein + e = **seine**
4. **Peter liebt seine Katze.**

So you make **two separate decisions**:
- **Who owns it?** → picks the word (mein, sein, ihr...)
- **What's the gender and case of the thing?** → picks the ending (-, -e, -en)

## More examples

| German                              | English                     |
| ----------------------------------- | --------------------------- |
| Das ist **mein** Bruder.            | That is my brother.         |
| Ich besuche **meinen** Bruder.      | I visit my brother.         |
| **Meine** Mutter ist Ärztin.        | My mother is a doctor.      |
| Wo ist **dein** Handy?              | Where is your phone?        |
| **Unsere** Kinder spielen.          | Our children are playing.   |
| Haben Sie **Ihren** Pass?           | Do you have your passport?  |

## Small spelling quirk: euer

When euer gets an ending, it drops the **-e-** in the middle:

- euer Hund (no ending, stays euer)
- **eure** Wohnung (not euere)
- **euren** Hund (not eueren)

## Common mistakes

| ❌ Wrong                        | ✅ Right                         | Why                                |
| ------------------------------ | ------------------------------- | ---------------------------------- |
| Ich besuche mein Bruder.       | Ich besuche meinen Bruder.      | der Bruder, object → -en           |
| Anna sucht seinen Schlüssel.   | Anna sucht ihren Schlüssel.     | Anna is "she" → ihr                |
| Peter liebt seinen Katze.      | Peter liebt seine Katze.        | ending follows die Katze → -e      |
| Mein Eltern wohnen hier.       | Meine Eltern wohnen hier.       | plural → -e                        |

## Practice

1. Das ist ___ (my) Vater.
2. Ich liebe ___ (my) Vater.
3. Wo ist ___ (your, du) Tasche? (die Tasche)
4. Er sucht ___ (his) Schlüssel. (der Schlüssel)
5. Maria besucht ___ (her) Eltern.
6. Wir verkaufen ___ (our) Auto. (das Auto)
7. Haben Sie ___ (your, formal) Ticket? (das Ticket)
8. Ich sehe ___ (your, ihr) Hund. (der Hund)

<summary>Answers</summary>

1. mein
2. meinen
3. deine
4. seinen
5. ihre (plural)
6. unser (neuter, no change)
7. Ihr (neuter, no change)
8. euren (euer + en, drop the -e-)

## Summary

- mein, dein, sein, ihr, sein, unser, euer, ihr, Ihr.
- **Same endings as ein**: masc. object → -en, feminine → -e, plural → -e.
- **Owner** picks the word. **Thing** picks the ending.
