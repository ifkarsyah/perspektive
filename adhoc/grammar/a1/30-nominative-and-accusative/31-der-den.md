# der and den

## The idea in one sentence

A masculine noun takes **der** when it does the action and **den** when the action is done to it. Feminine, neuter, and plural don't change.

## Compare with English

Think of **he** and **him**. German's der and den work the same way:

| English              | German                     |
| -------------------- | -------------------------- |
| **He** sees the dog. | **Der** Mann sieht den Hund. |
| The dog sees **him**. | Der Hund sieht **den** Mann. |

## Step 1: Find who is doing what

Ask two questions about the sentence:

1. **Who is doing it?** → nominative (subject)
2. **What or who is it being done to?** → accusative (direct object)

Example: "Der Mann kauft den Tisch." (The man buys the table.)

1. Who is buying? The man → **der Mann** = nominative
2. What is being bought? The table → **den Tisch** = accusative

## Step 2: Only masculine changes

| Case          | masc.     | fem.    | neut.   | plural  |
| ------------- | --------- | ------- | ------- | ------- |
| Nominative    | der       | die     | das     | die     |
| Accusative    | **den**   | die     | das     | die     |

So the only thing to remember: **der → den** when it's the object. Everything else stays the same.

- **Die** Frau liest. / Ich sehe **die** Frau.
- **Das** Kind spielt. / Ich sehe **das** Kind.
- **Die** Kinder spielen. / Ich sehe **die** Kinder.

This means you first need to know the noun's [gender](../20-noun-gender-and-plural/21-der-die-das.md). If it isn't masculine, you don't have to think about the case at all.

## Step 3: Verbs that often have an object

These verbs usually have an object, so a masculine noun after them takes **den**:

| Verb     | Example                    | English                  |
| -------- | -------------------------- | ------------------------ |
| haben    | Ich habe **den** Schlüssel. | I have the key.         |
| kaufen   | Ich kaufe **den** Tisch.   | I'm buying the table.    |
| sehen    | Ich sehe **den** Hund.     | I see the dog.           |
| trinken  | Ich trinke **den** Kaffee. | I'm drinking the coffee. |
| nehmen   | Wir nehmen **den** Bus.    | We're taking the bus.    |
| kennen   | Ich kenne **den** Lehrer.  | I know the teacher.      |
| suchen   | Ich suche **den** Schlüssel. | I'm looking for the key. |

## Step 4: After sein, there's no object

**sein** (ist, sind) doesn't do anything to anyone. It just says what something is. So the noun stays nominative:

- Das **ist der** Schlüssel. (That's the key.)
- Hier **ist der** Bahnhof. (The station is here.)

## Step 5: Don't trust the position

In English, the first noun is usually the subject. In German, the word decides, not the position.

The subject can come **after** the verb:

- Heute kommt **der** Arzt. (The doctor is coming today.)

And the object can come **first**:

- **Den** Film sehe ich heute. (I'm watching the film today.)
- **Den** Mann sieht der Hund. (The dog sees the man.)

The verb is still in seat 2 each time. Always ask the two questions from Step 1.

## Common mistakes

| ❌ Wrong                | ✅ Right                | Why                            |
| ---------------------- | ---------------------- | ------------------------------ |
| Ich kaufe der Tisch.   | Ich kaufe den Tisch.   | masculine object → den         |
| Den Tisch ist neu.     | Der Tisch ist neu.     | masculine subject → der        |
| Das ist den Schlüssel. | Das ist der Schlüssel. | after sein → der               |
| Ich kaufe den Tasche.  | Ich kaufe die Tasche.  | feminine doesn't change        |
| Ich lese den Buch.     | Ich lese das Buch.     | neuter doesn't change          |
| Ich sehe den Kinder.   | Ich sehe die Kinder.   | plural doesn't change          |

## Practice

Fill in der, den, die, or das.

1. ___ Hund schläft. (der Hund)
2. Ich kaufe ___ Tisch. (der Tisch)
3. Das ist ___ Schlüssel. (der Schlüssel)
4. Wir nehmen ___ Bus. (der Bus)
5. Heute kommt ___ Arzt. (der Arzt)
6. ___ Film sehe ich heute. (der Film)
7. Ich kaufe ___ Tasche. (die Tasche)
8. Ich lese ___ Buch. (das Buch)

Who is doing the seeing?

9. Der Hund sieht den Mann.

## Summary

- **der** = masculine subject. **den** = masculine object.
- Feminine, neuter, and plural look the same in both cases.
- Ask: who is doing it, and what is it being done to?
- After **sein**, use **der**.
- Trust the word, not the position.
