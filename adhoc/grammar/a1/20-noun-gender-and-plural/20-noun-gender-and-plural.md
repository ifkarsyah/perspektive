# Noun Gender and Plural

## What is this about?

In English, every noun just uses "the": the table, the lamp, the book.

In German, every noun belongs to one of **three groups**, called genders, and each group has its own word for "the":

| Group (gender) | "the" | Example          |
| -------------- | ----- | ---------------- |
| masculine      | **der** | der Tisch (table) |
| feminine       | **die** | die Lampe (lamp)  |
| neuter         | **das** | das Buch (book)   |

And when there's more than one, the noun changes its ending in one of several ways (one Buch, two Bücher).

## Why it matters

The gender isn't just about "the". It changes lots of other things later: the word for "a" (ein/eine), "my" (mein/meine), "it" (er/sie/es), and adjective endings. If you don't know a noun's gender, you'll get all of those wrong too.

## The good news and the bad news

**Bad news:** gender is mostly random. A table is masculine, a lamp is feminine, a book is neuter. There's no logic you can fully rely on.

**Good news:** there are useful hints, and the fix is simple: **always learn the noun together with its article and plural.** Never learn "Tisch". Learn "**der Tisch, die Tische**".
