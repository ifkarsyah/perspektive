Der Plural
Learn German · Topic 2 · Plural

One die, many endings

More than one? The article is always die. The noun itself changes in one of a handful of ways.

Compare with English

English's "irregular" plurals are German's normal ones

English: mostly -s

book → books
child → children · man → men · mouse → mice

The odd ones are exceptions.

German: about 5 patterns

Kind → Kinder · Apfel → Äpfel
Auto → Autos

Changes like "children" and "men" are everyday patterns. -s is the least common one.

The noun ending

Eight ways a noun can change

Five endings (-e, -er, -(e)n, -s, nothing), and some of them add an umlaut: the two dots on ä ö ü.

Rules of thumb

Guess the plural from two clues

Pick the gender and what the word looks like. The rules work most of the time, not always.

1 · Gender
2 · The word…
  1. Feminine → -n / -en (most reliable)
  2. Female person in -in → -nen
  3. Masc./neuter in -er, -el, -en → no ending, sometimes umlaut
  4. Short masculine → -e, often umlaut
  5. Short neuter → -er, umlaut if possible
  6. Ends in -a, -i, -o, -u or foreign → -s
"A" has no plural

ein disappears, just like "a"

Ich habe ein Kind.
I have a child.
Ich habe zwei Kinder.
I have two children.
Ich kaufe ein Äpfel.
I buy apples.
How to learn plurals

Every noun is three pieces

der Stuhl, die Stühlechair
Dictionaries write it like this
Stuhl,the noun der,masculine -¨eumlaut + e → Stühle

The dash stands for the noun. ¨ means "add an umlaut". So Kind, das, -er is Kinder, and Lehrer, der, - means no change.

Summary

What to keep

  • Plural article is always  die.
  • Five endings: -e, -er, -(e)n, -s, nothing. Often with an umlaut.
  • Feminine → usually -(e)n. Ends in -er/-el/-en → usually no change.
  • Learn every noun as article + singular + plural.
