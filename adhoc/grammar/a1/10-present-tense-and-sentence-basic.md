# Present Tense and Sentence Basic

## What is this about?

This is the very first building block: how to say simple things that happen **now**, like "I live in Berlin" or "Do you speak English?".

Good news: German has only **one** present tense. English has two ("I work" and "I am working"). German uses the same form for both.

| English              | German       |
| -------------------- | ------------ |
| I work.              | Ich arbeite. |
| I am working.        | Ich arbeite. |
| I'm working tomorrow. | Ich arbeite morgen. |

The last line shows something nice: German also uses the present tense for the future, as long as a time word (morgen = tomorrow) makes the meaning clear.

## Why it matters

Almost every sentence you say as a beginner is in the present tense. If you get this part right, you can already talk about yourself, your day, your job, and ask simple questions.

## The plan

You'll learn it in five small steps. Each step builds on the one before, so go in order:

1. [Regular verbs](11-regular-verbs.md): how verbs change their ending depending on who is doing the action (ich wohne, du wohnst...).
2. [sein and haben](12-sein-and-haben.md): the two most important verbs, "to be" and "to have". They don't follow the normal pattern, so they get their own lesson.
3. [Personal pronouns](13-personal-pronouns.md): the small words ich, du, er, sie... and the tricky choice between du and Sie.
4. [Verb in second position](14-verb-second-position.md): the single most important word order rule in German.
5. [Questions](15-questions.md): how to turn a sentence into a question.

When you finish these five, you can build sentences like:

> Ich heiße Anna. Ich bin 25 Jahre alt und wohne in Berlin. Heute habe ich Zeit. Hast du auch Zeit?
>
> (My name is Anna. I'm 25 and live in Berlin. Today I have time. Do you have time too?)
