# Separable Verbs

## What is this about?

Some German verbs come in **two pieces** that split apart in a sentence. One piece stays in seat 2, the other piece flies to the end.

- aufstehen (to get up) → Ich **stehe** um 7 Uhr **auf**.

## Compare with English

English has something very similar: **phrasal verbs**.

- get **up**, call **back**, turn **off**, pick **up**

And English splits them too, sometimes:

- I pick **you up**. (not "I pick up you")
- Turn **it off**.

German does the same thing, but **always**, and the little piece goes **all the way to the end**:

- Ich **hole** dich morgen um 8 Uhr **ab**. (I'll pick you up tomorrow at 8.)

## Why it matters

Many of the most common daily verbs are separable: getting up, shopping, calling, watching TV, arriving, coming along. You'll use them constantly to talk about your day.

## Connection to modal verbs

You already know the "sandwich" from [modal verbs](52-infinitive-at-the-end.md). Separable verbs make the same sandwich, just with the two halves of one verb:

- Ich **kann** heute **kommen**. (modal sandwich)
- Ich **komme** heute **mit**. (separable sandwich)
