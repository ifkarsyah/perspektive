# Time Expressions

## What is this about?

How to say **when** something happens: on Monday, at 8 o'clock, in July, every day, sometimes.

## Compare with English

English uses three little words: **on**, **at**, **in**.

- **on** Monday
- **at** 8 o'clock
- **in** July

German also uses three little words, and they match almost perfectly:

| English     | German  | Example                  |
| ----------- | ------- | ------------------------ |
| **on** Monday | **am** | **am** Montag          |
| **at** 8 o'clock | **um** | **um** 8 Uhr       |
| **in** July  | **im** | **im** Juli             |

That's the core of this topic. If you know am / um / im, you're 80% done.

## Why it matters

You can't make plans, book appointments, or describe your day without time expressions. And they combine nicely with [verb in second position](14-verb-second-position.md): starting a sentence with a time is very natural in German.

> Am Montag gehe ich zum Deutschkurs. Um 9 Uhr beginnt der Kurs.
