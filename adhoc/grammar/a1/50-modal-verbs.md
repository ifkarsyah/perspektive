# Modal Verbs

## What is this about?

Modal verbs are "helper" verbs that add a **mood** to another verb: ability, need, desire, or permission.

English has them too: **can**, **must**, **want to**, **would like to**, **may**.

| English              | German               |
| -------------------- | -------------------- |
| I **can** swim.      | Ich **kann** schwimmen.  |
| I **must** work.     | Ich **muss** arbeiten.   |
| I **want to** sleep. | Ich **will** schlafen.   |
| I **would like** a coffee. | Ich **möchte** einen Kaffee. |
| **May** I come in?   | **Darf** ich reinkommen? |

## Why it matters

Modal verbs are everywhere in daily life: ordering food (Ich möchte...), asking for help (Können Sie...?), saying what you have to do (Ich muss...). They're also your first taste of an important German pattern: **the verb bracket**.

## The big new thing: the second verb goes to the end

Look at the examples above again:

- Ich **kann** gut **schwimmen**.

In English, "can" and "swim" stand next to each other. In German, the modal verb stays in **seat 2**, and the main verb jumps to **the very end** of the sentence. Everything else goes in between, like a sandwich.
