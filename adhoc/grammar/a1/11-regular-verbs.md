# Regular Verbs

## The idea in one sentence

In German, the **end of the verb changes** depending on **who** is doing the action.

## Compare with English

English does this too, but only a tiny bit:

- I play
- you play
- he play**s** ← only this one changes

German does the same thing, just for **every** person:

- ich spiel**e**
- du spiel**st**
- er spiel**t**

So the idea isn't new. German is just more consistent about it.

## Step 1: Find the stem

Every verb in the dictionary ends in **-en** (this is called the infinitive, the "to ..." form).

- spiel**en** = to play
- wohn**en** = to live
- lern**en** = to learn

Cut off the -en. What's left is the **stem**:

- spielen → **spiel**
- wohnen → **wohn**
- lernen → **lern**

Think of the stem as the "body" of the verb. It never changes. Only the ending (the "tail") changes.

## Step 2: Add the ending for the person

| Who                    | Ending | spielen (to play) | wohnen (to live) |
| ---------------------- | ------ | ----------------- | ---------------- |
| ich (I)                | **-e**  | ich spiel**e**    | ich wohn**e**    |
| du (you, one friend)   | **-st** | du spiel**st**    | du wohn**st**    |
| er / sie / es (he/she/it) | **-t** | er spiel**t**   | sie wohn**t**    |
| wir (we)               | **-en** | wir spiel**en**   | wir wohn**en**   |
| ihr (you, a group of friends) | **-t** | ihr spiel**t** | ihr wohn**t** |
| sie / Sie (they / you formal) | **-en** | sie spiel**en** | Sie wohn**en** |

That's it. This one table works for most German verbs.

## How to remember the endings

Say them out loud as a little chant: **e - st - t - en - t - en**.

Some shortcuts:

- **wir** and **sie/Sie** always look exactly like the dictionary form. (wir spielen, sie spielen.) Free points.
- **er/sie/es** and **ihr** both get **-t**.
- **du** is the only one with an **s**: -st. Think "**s**t for **s**omeone you know".

## Worked example

Let's build "You learn German" (talking to a friend).

1. Who? "You" talking to a friend = **du**.
2. Verb? "learn" = lernen → stem **lern**.
3. Ending for du? **-st**.
4. Put it together: du + lern + st = **Du lernst Deutsch.**

Another one: "We live in Munich."

1. Who? **wir**.
2. Verb? wohnen → stem **wohn**.
3. Ending for wir? **-en**.
4. **Wir wohnen in München.**

## Two small exceptions (they're about pronunciation)

### Stem ends in -t or -d

Try to say "du arbeit**st**". Hard, right? Too many consonants in a row. So German adds an extra **-e-** to make it easy to say:

- arbeiten (to work): du arbeit**e**st, er arbeit**e**t, ihr arbeit**e**t
- finden (to find): du find**e**st, er find**e**t, ihr find**e**t

Only the du, er/sie/es, and ihr forms get the extra -e-. The rest are normal.

### Stem ends in -s, -ß, or -z

Try "du heiß**st**". The s sound is already there, so a second s is pointless. Just add **-t**:

- heißen (to be called): du heiß**t** (not heißst)
- tanzen (to dance): du tanz**t**

## Common mistakes

| ❌ Wrong          | ✅ Right         | Why                                      |
| ---------------- | --------------- | ---------------------------------------- |
| Ich spielen.     | Ich spiele.     | ich always takes -e, not the dictionary form |
| Er spiel.        | Er spielt.      | er/sie/es needs the -t                   |
| Du spielt.       | Du spielst.     | du needs -st, not just -t                |
| Du arbeitst.     | Du arbeitest.   | stem ends in -t, so add an extra -e-     |

## Summary

- Verb = **stem** + **ending**.
- Endings: ich **-e**, du **-st**, er/sie/es **-t**, wir **-en**, ihr **-t**, sie/Sie **-en**.
- Stem ends in -t/-d → add **-e-** (du arbeit**e**st).
- Stem ends in -s/-ß/-z → du just gets **-t** (du heißt).
