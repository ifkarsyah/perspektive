# der/die/das

## The idea in one sentence

Every German noun is **masculine (der)**, **feminine (die)**, or **neuter (das)**, and you have to learn which one together with the word.

## Gender is about the word, not the thing

This is the confusing part. "Masculine" doesn't mean the thing is male. It's just the name of a group.

| Noun            | Gender     | Is it logical?                    |
| --------------- | ---------- | --------------------------------- |
| der Mann (man)  | masculine  | ✅ yes                             |
| die Frau (woman)| feminine   | ✅ yes                             |
| der Tisch (table) | masculine | 🤷 no reason                     |
| die Lampe (lamp) | feminine  | 🤷 no reason                      |
| das Mädchen (girl) | **neuter** | ❌ a girl is "it"! (explained below) |

Think of der, die, das as three **boxes** that every noun has been put into. Sometimes the box makes sense, often it doesn't.

## The golden rule

**Never learn a noun without its article.**

- ❌ Tisch = table
- ✅ **der** Tisch = table

Say it out loud as one word: "dertisch", "dielampe", "dasbuch". Your ear will start to remember what sounds right.

### Color trick

Many learners color-code their flashcards:

- 🔵 **der** = blue
- 🔴 **die** = red
- 🟢 **das** = green

Your brain remembers colors better than letters. Later, you'll "see" der Tisch as blue.

## Hints: endings that tell you the gender

You can't guess every noun, but these endings are right most of the time. Look at the **end of the word**:

### Usually der (masculine)

| Clue                         | Examples                          |
| ---------------------------- | --------------------------------- |
| male people, jobs ending -er | der Vater, der Lehrer, der Bäcker |
| tools ending in -er          | der Computer, der Fernseher       |
| days, months, seasons        | der Montag, der Mai, der Sommer   |
| -ling                        | der Frühling, der Liebling        |
| -ismus                       | der Tourismus                     |

### Usually die (feminine)

| Clue                          | Examples                           |
| ----------------------------- | ---------------------------------- |
| female people                 | die Mutter, die Lehrerin           |
| -ung                          | die Zeitung, die Wohnung           |
| -heit, -keit                  | die Freiheit, die Möglichkeit      |
| -schaft                       | die Freundschaft                   |
| -ion, -tät                    | die Nation, die Universität        |
| most nouns ending in **-e**   | die Lampe, die Straße, die Tasche  |

### Usually das (neuter)

| Clue                          | Examples                           |
| ----------------------------- | ---------------------------------- |
| **-chen, -lein** (always!)    | das Mädchen, das Brötchen          |
| -ment, -um                    | das Dokument, das Zentrum          |
| verbs used as nouns           | das Essen (eating/food), das Leben (life) |

**Why is das Mädchen neuter?** Because **-chen** means "little" and always makes a word neuter. Mädchen literally means "little maid". The ending wins over the meaning.

## Compound nouns: look at the last word

German loves to glue nouns together. The **last word decides** the gender:

- das Haus (house) + **die Tür** (door) = **die** Haustür (front door)
- die Hand (hand) + **der Schuh** (shoe) = **der** Handschuh (glove)
- der Kaffee (coffee) + **die Maschine** = **die** Kaffeemaschine

So if you know the last word, you know the whole word.

## Common mistakes

| ❌ Wrong          | ✅ Right          | Why                                |
| ---------------- | ---------------- | ---------------------------------- |
| die Mädchen (one girl) | das Mädchen | -chen is always das               |
| der Zeitung      | die Zeitung      | -ung is always die                 |
| das Haustür      | die Haustür      | last word (die Tür) decides        |
| learning "Auto = car" | "das Auto = car" | always learn the article       |

## Practice

Guess the article using the hints.

1. ___ Wohnung (apartment)
2. ___ Brötchen (bread roll)
3. ___ Dienstag (Tuesday)
4. ___ Freiheit (freedom)
5. ___ Tasche (bag)
6. ___ Lehrer (male teacher)
7. ___ Schreibtisch (desk: schreiben + der Tisch)
8. ___ Universität

1. die (-ung)
2. das (-chen)
3. der (day of the week)
4. die (-heit)
5. die (ends in -e)
6. der (male person)
7. der (last word is der Tisch)
8. die (-tät)

## Summary

- Three genders: **der** (masculine), **die** (feminine), **das** (neuter).
- Gender is mostly random. **Always learn the article with the noun.**
- Endings help: -ung/-heit/-keit/-e → die, -chen → das, days/months → der.
- Compound nouns: the **last** word decides.
