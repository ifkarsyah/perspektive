# Verb in Second Position

## The idea in one sentence

In a normal German sentence, the **verb is always the second thing**. Not the second word, the second **thing**.

This is the single most important word order rule in German. Get this right and your sentences will sound German.

## Compare with English

In English, the subject (I, you, my brother) almost always comes right before the verb:

- **I drink** coffee in the morning.
- In the morning **I drink** coffee.

In the second sentence, "In the morning" comes first, then "I", then "drink". The verb is in position 3. English doesn't care.

**German cares.** The verb must stay in position 2:

- **Ich trinke** morgens Kaffee.
- Morgens **trinke ich** Kaffee.

See what happened? When "Morgens" (in the morning) takes position 1, the verb **stays** in position 2, and "ich" has to **move behind** the verb.

## Think of it like seats in a row

Imagine every sentence is a row of seats. Seat number 2 is reserved for the verb. Always.

| Seat 1          | Seat 2 (verb!) | Everything else     |
| --------------- | -------------- | ------------------- |
| Ich             | **trinke**     | morgens Kaffee.     |
| Morgens         | **trinke**     | ich Kaffee.         |
| Kaffee          | **trinke**     | ich morgens.        |

All three sentences are correct German. They mean the same thing, just with a different emphasis. The only thing that never moves is the verb in seat 2.

## What counts as "one thing"?

Seat 1 can hold **one word or a whole group of words** that belong together:

| Seat 1                 | Seat 2  | Rest                |
| ---------------------- | ------- | ------------------- |
| Ich                    | **gehe** | heute ins Kino.    |
| Heute                  | **gehe** | ich ins Kino.      |
| Am Montag              | **gehe** | ich ins Kino.      |
| Mein kleiner Bruder    | **geht** | heute ins Kino.    |
| Nach der Arbeit        | **gehe** | ich ins Kino.      |

"Am Montag" (on Monday) is two words, but it's one piece of information: a time. So it fills seat 1 alone.

## The flip (inversion)

When something that is **not** the subject goes first, the subject jumps behind the verb. This is called **inversion**.

Step by step: "Today I work."

1. Start with the normal order: Ich arbeite heute.
2. Move "heute" to the front: Heute ... 
3. Verb stays in seat 2: Heute **arbeite** ...
4. Subject goes right after the verb: Heute arbeite **ich**.

✅ Heute arbeite ich.
❌ Heute ich arbeite. (two things before the verb: wrong!)

This is the #1 mistake English speakers make. Watch out for it.

## Words that don't take a seat

The words **und** (and), **aber** (but), **oder** (or), **denn** (because), **sondern** (but rather) just connect two sentences. They sit in "seat 0" and don't count:

| Seat 0 | Seat 1    | Seat 2     | Rest                |
| ------ | --------- | ---------- | ------------------- |
|        | Ich       | **lerne**  | Deutsch,            |
| aber   | mein Bruder | **lernt** | Spanisch.          |

Ich lerne Deutsch, **aber** mein Bruder **lernt** Spanisch. (not "aber lernt mein Bruder")

## Preview: when there are two verbs

Sometimes a sentence has two verb parts. Then the conjugated one goes in seat 2, and the other one goes to the **very end**. It looks like a bracket around the sentence:

- Ich **kann** sehr gut **kochen**. (I can cook very well.)
- Ich **stehe** um 7 Uhr **auf**. (I get up at 7.)

You'll learn this properly in [modal verbs](52-infinitive-at-the-end.md) and [separable verbs](../60-separable-verbs.md). For now, just know seat 2 is still the verb.

## Common mistakes

| ❌ Wrong                        | ✅ Right                         | Why                              |
| ------------------------------ | ------------------------------- | -------------------------------- |
| Heute ich gehe ins Kino.       | Heute gehe ich ins Kino.        | verb must be in seat 2           |
| Am Wochenende wir spielen Fußball. | Am Wochenende spielen wir Fußball. | same: flip subject and verb |
| Aber spielt er Gitarre.        | Aber er spielt Gitarre.         | aber doesn't take a seat         |
| Morgens trinke Kaffee ich.     | Morgens trinke ich Kaffee.      | subject comes right after the verb |

## Practice

Rewrite each sentence, starting with the **bold** part.

1. Ich spiele **am Samstag** Tennis.
2. Wir essen **heute** Pizza.
3. Er wohnt **seit 2020** in Berlin.
4. Ich trinke **abends** Tee.
5. Sie arbeitet **in München**.
6. Is this correct? "Morgen ich fahre nach Hamburg."

<details>
<summary>Answers</summary>

1. Am Samstag spiele ich Tennis.
2. Heute essen wir Pizza.
3. Seit 2020 wohnt er in Berlin.
4. Abends trinke ich Tee.
5. In München arbeitet sie.
6. No. Morgen **fahre ich** nach Hamburg.

</details>

## Summary

- The verb is always in **seat 2**.
- Seat 1 can be the subject, a time, a place, or anything else (one "thing").
- If seat 1 isn't the subject, the subject goes **right after the verb** (inversion).
- und, aber, oder, denn, sondern don't take a seat.
