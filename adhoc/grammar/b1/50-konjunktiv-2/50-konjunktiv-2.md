# Konjunktiv II

## What is this about?

At A2 you learned a few special forms for being [polite](../../a2/90-polite-requests.md): hätte, könnte, würde. Those were Konjunktiv II, even though we didn't need the name.

The same forms have a bigger job: talking about things that **aren't real**. Things you imagine, wish for, or regret.

- Wenn ich Zeit **hätte**, **würde** ich kommen. (If I had time, I would come.)
- Ich **wäre** gern in Japan. (I'd love to be in Japan.)
- Das **hätte** ich nicht gedacht. (I wouldn't have thought that.)

## Compare with English

English does the same thing. It takes a past-looking form and adds "would":

| Real                          | Not real                         |
| ----------------------------- | -------------------------------- |
| If I **have** time, I **will** come. | If I **had** time, I **would** come. |
| Wenn ich Zeit **habe**, **komme** ich. | Wenn ich Zeit **hätte**, **würde** ich **kommen**. |

"If I had time" doesn't mean the past. It means "I don't have time, but imagine I did." German **hätte** works the same way.

## The big rule: two kinds of verbs

Good news: you only need a small set of forms.

1. **sein, haben, and the modal verbs** have their own forms: wäre, hätte, könnte, müsste...
2. **Every other verb** uses **würde + infinitive**: würde kommen, würde kaufen, würde machen.

## Why it matters

This is how you talk about dreams, give advice, and express regret. It also makes you sound softer and less blunt, which matters a lot in German conversation.

- **Was würdest du machen**, wenn du im Lotto gewinnen würdest? (What would you do if you won the lottery?)
- **An deiner Stelle würde** ich das nicht machen. (If I were you, I wouldn't do that.)
- **Ich würde sagen**, das stimmt nicht. (I'd say that's not right.)
