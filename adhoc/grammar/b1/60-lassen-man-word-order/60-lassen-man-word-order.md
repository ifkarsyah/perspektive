# lassen, man, and Word Order

## What is this about?

These are three small tools that make your German sound much more natural.

- **lassen**: to get something done, to let someone do something, or to leave something.
- **man**: "you", "people", or "one" in general.
- **Word order** in the middle of a sentence: where to put time, place, and pronouns.

## Compare with English

| Tool       | English                       | German                           |
| ---------- | ----------------------------- | -------------------------------- |
| lassen     | I'm **having** my car **repaired**. | Ich **lasse** mein Auto **reparieren**. |
| lassen     | **Let's** go!                 | **Lass uns** gehen!              |
| man        | How do **you** say that?      | Wie sagt **man** das?            |
| Word order | I'm going to Berlin tomorrow. | Ich fahre **morgen** nach Berlin. |

Notice the last one: English puts the place first, while German puts the time first.

## Why it matters

None of these are hard, and you'll use all three every day.

- **Lass uns** einen Kaffee trinken! (Let's have a coffee!)
- Wie schreibt **man** das? (How do you spell that?)
- Ich gebe **es dir** morgen. (I'll give it to you tomorrow.)
