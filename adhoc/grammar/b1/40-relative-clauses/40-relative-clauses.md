# Relative Clauses

## What is this about?

A relative clause describes a noun using a whole mini-sentence.

- Das ist der Mann. Er wohnt neben mir.
- Das ist der Mann, **der** neben mir **wohnt**. (That's the man who lives next to me.)

## Compare with English

English uses **who**, **which**, or **that**. German uses words that look just like **der, die, das**:

| English                    | German                         |
| -------------------------- | ------------------------------ |
| the man **who** lives here | der Mann, **der** hier wohnt   |
| the book **that** I'm reading | das Buch, **das** ich lese  |

There's one important difference. English can drop the word; German **never** can:

- the film (that) I saw → der Film, **den** ich gesehen habe

## The big rule: comma, pronoun, verb at the end

A relative clause is a [subordinate clause](../../a2/50-subordinate-clauses/52-verb-at-the-end.md), so everything you know about those applies:

- It's separated by a **comma**.
- The conjugated verb goes to the **end**.

| Noun       | Comma + pronoun | Middle      | Verb at the end |
| ---------- | --------------- | ----------- | --------------- |
| der Mann   | , **der**       | neben mir   | **wohnt**       |
| der Film   | , **den**       | ich gestern | **gesehen habe** |

## Why it matters

Relative clauses let you say exactly who or what you mean, in one sentence:

- Kennst du die Frau, **die** dort steht? (Do you know the woman standing over there?)
- Ich suche eine Wohnung, **die** nicht zu teuer ist. (I'm looking for an apartment that isn't too expensive.)

And they're a rescue tool. When you forget a word, you can describe it:

- Ich suche das Ding, **mit dem** man Flaschen öffnet. (I'm looking for the thing you open bottles with.)
