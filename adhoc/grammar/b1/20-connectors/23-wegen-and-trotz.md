# wegen and trotz

## The idea in one sentence

**wegen** (because of) and **trotz** (despite) say the same thing as weil and obwohl, but with just a noun instead of a whole clause.

## Compare with English

English has the same two options:

| With a clause                 | With a noun            |
| ----------------------------- | ---------------------- |
| **because** it's snowing      | **because of** the snow |
| **although** it's raining     | **despite** the rain   |
| **weil** es schneit           | **wegen** dem Schnee   |
| **obwohl** es regnet          | **trotz** dem Regen    |

## Why it matters

They're short, and they're great for quick replies:

- Warum kommst du so spät? — **Wegen dem Stau.** (Why are you late? Because of the traffic.)
- Warum kommst du nicht? — **Wegen der Arbeit.** (Why aren't you coming? Because of work.)

## Step 1: In conversation, use the dative

These two prepositions officially take the **genitive**, which you'll see in writing. In everyday speech, most people use the **dative**, which you already know.

| Noun     | What you'll say       | What you'll read          |
| -------- | --------------------- | ------------------------- |
| der Stau | wegen **dem** Stau    | wegen **des** Staus       |
| das Wetter | wegen **dem** Wetter | wegen **des** Wetters    |
| die Arbeit | wegen **der** Arbeit | wegen **der** Arbeit     |
| der Regen | trotz **dem** Regen  | trotz **des** Regens      |

Notice that feminine nouns look the same either way.

Some Germans consider the dative here a little careless, but in conversation it's completely normal. In a formal email, use the genitive form. You can look it up when you need it.

### Often there's no article at all

- **Wegen** Krankheit geschlossen. (Closed due to illness.)
- **Trotz** Regen sind wir spazieren gegangen. (We went for a walk despite the rain.)

## Step 2: With people

Use a dative pronoun in speech:

- Ich bin **wegen dir** gekommen. (I came because of you.)
- Das ist **wegen mir** passiert. (That happened because of me.)

## Step 3: Word order

**wegen** and **trotz** are prepositions, so they don't move any verbs. The whole phrase counts as one thing. If it comes first, it takes seat 1:

- **Wegen dem Stau** **kommen** wir zu spät. (We're late because of the traffic.)
- Wir kommen **wegen dem Stau** zu spät.
- **Trotz dem Regen** **gehen** wir spazieren.

Compare them with weil, which does send the verb to the end:

- Wir kommen zu spät, **weil** es einen Stau **gibt**.

## Step 4: Change a clause into a noun

| With a clause                      | With a noun                     |
| ---------------------------------- | ------------------------------- |
| Weil es schneit, ...               | Wegen dem Schnee ...            |
| Weil ich viel arbeiten muss, ...   | Wegen der Arbeit ...            |
| Obwohl es regnet, ...              | Trotz dem Regen ...             |
| Obwohl es kalt ist, ...            | Trotz der Kälte ...             |

## A memory trick

**trotz** + **dem** = **trotzdem**. Once upon a time it meant "despite that", and it still does.

## In conversation

> **Kim:** Du bist aber spät dran!
> **Alex:** Ja, sorry. **Wegen dem** Stau.
> **Kim:** Schon wieder? Kommst du morgen mit dem Fahrrad?
> **Alex:** Ja, **trotz dem** Regen.

## Common mistakes

| ❌ Wrong                            | ✅ Right                               | Why                           |
| ---------------------------------- | -------------------------------------- | ----------------------------- |
| wegen es regnet                    | weil es regnet                         | a clause needs weil           |
| weil dem Stau                      | wegen dem Stau                         | a noun needs wegen            |
| trotz es regnet                    | obwohl es regnet                       | a clause needs obwohl         |
| Wegen dem Stau wir kommen zu spät. | Wegen dem Stau kommen wir zu spät.     | the phrase takes seat 1       |
| Wegen dem Stau, kommen wir zu spät. | Wegen dem Stau kommen wir zu spät.    | no comma after the phrase     |

## Practice

Fill in wegen, weil, trotz, or obwohl.

1. Ich bleibe zu Hause, ___ ich krank bin.

3. Wir gehen spazieren, ___ es kalt ist.
4. Wir gehen ___ der Kälte spazieren.

Say it with a noun.

5. Weil es einen Stau gibt, komme ich zu spät. (der Stau)

Answer briefly with wegen.

## Summary

- **weil / obwohl** + clause. **wegen / trotz** + noun.
- In conversation, use the **dative**: wegen **dem** Stau.
- In writing, you'll see the genitive: wegen **des** Staus.
- They don't move the verb. If the phrase comes first, the verb comes right after.
