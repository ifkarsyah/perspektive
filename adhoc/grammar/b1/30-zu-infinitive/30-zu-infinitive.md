# Infinitive with zu

## What is this about?

You already know how to put a second verb at the end after a [modal verb](../../a1/50-modal-verbs/52-infinitive-at-the-end.md):

- Ich **kann** Deutsch **sprechen**.

But after most other verbs, the second verb needs a little word first: **zu**.

- Ich **versuche**, Deutsch **zu sprechen**. (I'm trying to speak German.)
- Hast du **Lust**, ins Kino **zu gehen**? (Do you feel like going to the cinema?)
- Es ist **schön**, dich **zu sehen**. (It's nice to see you.)

## Compare with English

German **zu** is like English **to** in "to speak". The difference is where everything else goes.

- English: I'm trying **to speak** German. (the object comes after)
- German: Ich versuche, Deutsch **zu sprechen**. (the object comes before)

## The big rule: zu + verb goes to the END

Just like the infinitive after a modal verb, **zu + infinitive** closes the sentence. Everything else goes in front of it:

- Ich habe keine Zeit, **zu kochen**.
- Ich habe keine Zeit, heute Abend **zu kochen**.
- Ich habe keine Zeit, heute Abend für alle **zu kochen**.

## Why it matters

This is how you suggest things, say what you're trying to do, and give your opinion. And it lets you say why you're doing something, with **um...zu**.

- **Hast du Lust**, mitzukommen? (Do you feel like coming along?)
- **Es ist wichtig**, viel zu sprechen. (It's important to speak a lot.)
- Ich lerne Deutsch, **um** in Berlin **zu arbeiten**. (I'm learning German in order to work in Berlin.)
