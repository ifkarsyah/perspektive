# Grammar for Speaking

You can get surprisingly far in spoken German with a small core of grammar. The list below is in rough priority order.

## Tier 1: The Essentials

1. **Present tense**: regular verbs, plus the irregular ones you use constantly: *sein, haben, gehen, machen, wissen, fahren, sehen, geben*.
2. **Word order**: in a main clause the verb always comes second (*Heute **gehe** ich ins Büro*). In yes/no questions the verb comes first (***Kommst** du mit?*), and in W-questions it comes right after the question word (*Wann **kommst** du?*). Wrong verb position is the biggest reason a sentence "sounds wrong".
3. **Modal verbs**: *können, müssen, wollen, möchten, dürfen, sollen*, with the second verb at the end as an infinitive (*Ich **kann** morgen **kommen***).
4. **Noun gender**: always learn a noun together with its article (*der Tisch, die Tür, das Fenster*), never the noun alone.
5. **Nominative vs accusative**: the main change is masculine *der → den*, *ein → einen* (*Ich sehe **den** Mann. Ich habe **einen** Hund.*). Feminine and neuter stay the same.
6. **Negation**: *nicht* for verbs and adjectives (*Ich komme nicht. Das ist nicht teuer.*), *kein* for nouns (*Ich habe kein Auto.*).
7. **Perfekt**: the past tense of speech, *haben/sein* + past participle at the end (*Ich habe gegessen. Ich bin gegangen.*). The exceptions are *war, hatte, konnte, wollte, musste*: use their simple past forms instead. You can skip the Präteritum of every other verb.
8. **Separable verbs**: the prefix goes to the end (*aufstehen → Ich **stehe** um 7 Uhr **auf***).

## Tier 2: Sounding Much Better

1. **Connectors**:
   - *und, aber, oder, denn*: normal word order (*Ich bleibe zu Hause, **denn** ich bin müde.*)
   - *weil, dass, wenn, ob*: the verb goes to the end (*Ich bleibe zu Hause, **weil** ich müde **bin**.*)
2. **Polite requests**: *Ich möchte..., Ich hätte gern..., Könnten Sie...?, Würden Sie...?*
3. **Dative basics**, learned as set phrases first:
   - *mit, bei, zu, von, nach, aus* + dative (*mit dem Bus, zum Bahnhof, nach Hause*)
   - *mir, dir, Ihnen* (*Wie geht es Ihnen? Das gefällt mir.*)
4. **Prepositions of place and time**: *in, an, auf, um, am, im* (*um 8 Uhr, am Montag, im Mai, in der Stadt*).
5. **Reflexive verbs**: *sich freuen, sich treffen, sich setzen* (*Ich freue mich.*).

## What You Can Skip for Now

- **Genitive**: in speech people use *von* instead (*das Auto von meinem Bruder*).
- **Passive, relative clauses, Konjunktiv I, Plusquamperfekt**: rare in everyday conversation.
- **Adjective endings**: Germans understand you fine when you get them wrong.
- **Full case tables**: see below.

## A Common Misconception

Many learners think they must master all four cases before they can speak. You don't. Case mistakes rarely block understanding, while wrong verb position and missing vocabulary do. Spend more time on high-frequency verbs, set phrases, and listening than on grammar tables.

## How to Practice

Knowing a grammar table is not the goal. The goal is producing the right form **without thinking about it** while you speak. That only comes from saying things out loud, fast, many times. Each topic file has a **Practices** section with drills for that topic. The rules and general drills below apply to all of them.

### Rules for every drill

- **Say it out loud.** Reading silently doesn't train your mouth.
- **Speed over perfection.** If you stop to think, the form isn't automatic yet, so repeat that item.
- **Short and daily.** 10 minutes every day works far better than 1 hour once a week.
- **Use your own sentences.** Sentences about your own life stick better than textbook ones.

### General drills

1. **Self-talk narration**: during everyday tasks, describe in German what you are doing, and what the people around you are doing (*Ich mache Kaffee. Er liest ein Buch.*). Use the structure from the topic you're currently studying.
2. **Personal script**: write 10 sentences about yourself (name, origin, job, where you live, hobbies, daily routine). Say them aloud until they come out fluently. Each time you finish a topic, add or rewrite a few sentences that use it, and retell the script about a friend (*Er heißt..., er wohnt...*).
3. **Record and check**: once a week, record yourself talking for 1 minute about your day. Listen back, check it against the **Common mistakes** table of each topic you've studied, and add every mistake you find to that topic's drills.

### Daily routine (about 10 minutes)

| When | What |
|---|---|
| Every day, 5 min | Drills from your **current topic's** Practices section |
| Every day, 3 min | One drill from an **earlier topic** (rotate through them) |
| Every day, 2 min | Personal script, out loud |
| During the day | Self-talk narration (no extra time) |
| Once a week | Record and check |

Move to the next topic when you pass the current topic's **Ready to move on** check. Keep the earlier topics in the review rotation so they stay automatic.

## Benchmark

If you can handle Tier 1 plus the connectors from Tier 2, you are at roughly a solid A2, which is enough for most daily conversations.
